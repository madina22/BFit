# BFit

### A Research-Based Mental Health Support App Addressing Eating Disorders

**BFit** is a research-based mental health support application designed to provide accessible, anonymous, and supportive resources for people experiencing difficulties related to eating, body image, and emotional well-being.

The project was developed with a focus on creating a safe digital environment where users can access educational materials, track their well-being, and connect with others without having to publicly reveal their identity.

> **Achievement:** 3rd Place among approximately 100 participating teams

---

## Problem

Eating disorders and unhealthy relationships with food and body image can affect a person's physical and psychological well-being. However, many people may hesitate to seek support because of stigma, lack of accessible information, or fear of judgment.

BFit was created to explore how a digital platform could provide an additional layer of support by combining:

* Mental health education
* Self-reflection tools
* Accessible psychological resources
* Anonymous peer support
* A user-friendly and non-judgmental environment

BFit is **not intended to replace professional medical or psychological treatment**. Instead, it aims to provide supportive resources and encourage users to seek appropriate professional help when needed.

---

## Our Solution

BFit combines several support features within a single application.

The platform was structured around **four core sections**, each addressing a different aspect of mental health and eating-disorder awareness.

### 1. Educational Resources

A collection of research-based educational materials covering topics related to:

* Eating disorders
* Body image
* Healthy relationships with food
* Emotional well-being
* Common misconceptions and warning signs

The goal was to make complex information easier to understand and access.

### 2. Self-Reflection & Tracking

Users can reflect on their emotional state and general well-being through simple interactive tools.

The section focuses on helping users recognize patterns in their feelings and experiences rather than encouraging restrictive behaviors or appearance-based comparisons.

### 3. Anonymous Peer-Support Community

BFit includes an anonymous community where users can share experiences, thoughts, and encouragement without publicly revealing their identity.

The community was designed around:

* Anonymity
* Empathy
* Non-judgmental communication
* Peer encouragement
* Supportive discussions

### 4. Support & Resources

The application provides information about seeking additional help and encourages users to involve qualified professionals when necessary.

This section was designed to make professional and external support easier to understand and access.

---

## Research-Based Approach

Research was an important part of the development process.

Before designing the application, we explored existing information and research surrounding eating disorders, mental health support, body image, and digital health interventions.

The research helped us determine:

* What problems the application should address
* Which types of information could be useful to users
* How to structure the application
* What principles should guide the peer-support community
* Which features could provide meaningful support without replacing professional care

The project therefore focused not only on developing an application, but also on understanding the problem it was designed to address.

---

## Target Users

BFit was primarily designed for people who:

* Want to learn more about eating disorders and mental well-being
* Experience difficulties with body image or their relationship with food
* Are looking for accessible mental health resources
* Want to reflect on their emotional well-being
* Would benefit from anonymous peer support

The application was designed with accessibility and privacy in mind.

## Privacy & Safety

Because BFit addresses a sensitive topic, privacy and safety were important considerations during the design process.

The concept emphasizes:

* Anonymous participation
* Non-judgmental communication
* Responsible mental-health information
* Avoiding harmful or triggering content
* Encouraging professional support when appropriate

BFit should be viewed as a **support and educational concept**, not as a diagnostic or treatment tool.
